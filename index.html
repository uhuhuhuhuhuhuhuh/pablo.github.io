How IC2.1 represents and learns from files

Recipe: zero, constant-byte, repeated-pattern and byte-counter regions are represented as deterministic instructions.

Reference: a repeated chunk can point backward to bytes already present in the same manifest.

Delta: a near-match to a previous chunk can be stored as a compact XOR or prefix/suffix patch.

Learned dictionary: useful samples from prior files are kept locally by file profile. The encoder may use those bytes as a Zstandard dictionary, but if the cumulative gain does not pay for embedding that dictionary, IC2.1 automatically falls back to the ordinary self-contained representation.

Literal: novel data is Zstandard-compressed when the pinned WASM codec is available, otherwise gzip is tried, and incompressible bytes remain raw.

The random 128-bit salt makes repeat shares look different. It is discarded by the decoder and is not encryption. The public token is capped at 1,500,000 characters for browser safety, but a much larger source can fit when recipes, deduplication, deltas, learned dictionaries, or compression make the exact descriptor small enough.
